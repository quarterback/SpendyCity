IMPLICATIONS · ISSUE 01

What the seven funds, taken together, suggest

A short essay. The structural pattern is consistent enough across instruments and stewards that it is worth naming.

1. Voter-restricted does not mean voter-restricted

The seven funds on this site were enacted under a range of restriction classes — voter-restricted by ballot measure, charter-restricted, enabling-act-restricted. In practice, restriction is not a property of the fund. It is a property of the council, the bureau, and the ordinances that follow enactment. Each of the seven shows the same arc: a narrow charge, an accumulating balance, an ordinance that broadens the charge, and a balance that flows under the new definition.

2. The structural pattern is independent of instrument

It does not matter whether the fund is a flat per-adult tax (Arts), a gross-receipts surcharge (PCEF), a real-estate excise, a dedicated property levy, or a county-wide marginal income tax (PFA, SHS). The accumulation curve is the same shape. The drift curve is the same shape. This suggests that the pattern is not a property of any one bureau or any one revenue mechanism. It is a property of how restricted funds are governed in this jurisdiction.

3. Carryover is the governance event

In every fund on this site, the policy event that created drift was the moment a multi-year carryover became unavoidable. Carryover is treated, in bureau memos, as a neutral fact — a balance to be allocated. It is not neutral. It is a governable surplus that creates standing pressure to redefine eligible uses. The mechanical link between under-spending and scope-broadening is visible across the seven.

4. Audit findings are absorbed, not enforced

The City Auditor and the County Auditor have, across the seven funds, produced findings that name the structural problem in plain language: collection-cost overruns, scope expansions, surplus carryover, compliance-rate gaps. The bureau response, in nearly every case, is to amend the definition that produced the finding rather than to change the practice. Audit becomes a documentation function, not an enforcement function.

5. The remedy is structural, not editorial

A site like this one is a documentation function as well, and is subject to the same gravitational pull. The remedy is in the enabling code: a sunset on each restricted fund, a public spend-down plan tied to verifiable beneficiary outcomes, and a hard ceiling on carryover beyond which the council is required to either disburse or return.

Authorial position

This site is published as journalism, not as advocacy. The implications above are observations from the data on the seven fund pages. They are not a proposal. They are not a campaign. The reader is the one who decides what, if anything, to do with them.
